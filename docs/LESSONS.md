# What we got wrong, and what fixing it taught us

The record of self-corrections in FlowState v2 (Track A2 of docs/ROADMAP.md).
Each entry names the mistake, how it was caught, what replaced it, and the
artifact that proves the replacement. Judges, reviewers and collaborators
should read this before the results documents: a project that documents its
corrections is easier to trust than one that reports only passes.

| # | What was wrong | How it was caught | What replaced it | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **v1's phantom-jam claim.** The first FlowState dissipated hand-seeded shocks in a first-order LWR model and called it phantom-jam control. LWR is string-stable by construction and cannot form stop-and-go waves; the ">15% AV penetration causes human gap exploitation" result had no vehicles, gaps or lanes to come from. | External audit, then the ADR-1 analysis. | Two tiers: SUMO + IDM as the primary engine where waves are emergent; the LWR engine kept as a labeled screening tier that may not back a validation report. | CLAUDE.md §1, ADR-1; `macrosim` outputs carry `tier="screening"`. |
| 2 | **PI-with-saturation specified as `v_target = 0.75 · platoon mean`.** A simplification that does not appear in Stern et al. (2018). On an open corridor it ratchets the AV's own target down: 94% throughput collapse in the M3 sweep. | The M3 sweep result looked like a controller failure; re-reading the paper's Eqs. (3)–(5) showed the spec was wrong. | The faithful controller (own-speed mean + bounded, non-negative gap catch-up): σ_v −29.7% with no resolved throughput cost. The simplification survives as `pi_meanfrac`, labeled. | docs/PI_CONTROLLER_FIX.md, `artifacts/pi_retune_summary.json`. |
| 3 | **JAD's bimodal results under a perfect oracle.** 5 of 20 seeds ended worse than no control; one went from 1 to 11 waves. | Per-seed inspection: the perfect oracle fires the instant any bin qualifies, the AV completes slow-in/hold/fast-out before the front arrives, re-triggers, and each fast-out can seed a secondary wave. | The oracle became swappable and every headline JAD result is reported under realistic latency (30–60 s) and ±20% noise, where no seed is worse than baseline. | docs/JAD_ORACLE_RESULTS.md, `OracleSpec`. |
| 4 | **The wave-speed criterion failure on US-101 read as a calibration defect.** | Newell's `(s0 + L)/T` on the fitted parameters predicted an in-band wave; a ring at 60 veh/km with the same fleet gave 14.6 km/h — matching the independently fitted FD's `w = −14.6 km/h`. | The failure is a site-length and operating-density artifact; the criterion still fails on the 640 m site and says so; the prediction that a long congested corridor passes became a falsifiable test on I-24, confirmed there on 2026-09-05 with the criterion's slant-stack estimator (15.7–15.9 km/h simulated, 19.9 observed) and not with the standard detector (8–10 km/h; docs/I24_VALIDATION.md §0.4). | docs/WAVE_SPEED_DIAGNOSIS.md, `artifacts/wave_speed_sitelength.json`. |
| 5 | **"Smoothing is free."** On the synthetic corridor FollowerStopper had no resolved throughput cost and saved fuel. | Re-running the penetration ladder on real 5-lane US-101 geometry with a calibrated fleet. | The σ_v dose-response replicates; the no-cost part does not (resolved −0.3…−1.6% throughput, +1.4…+2.7% fuel). "For free" is corridor-dependent and is now said that way. | docs/US101_PENETRATION.md. |
| 6 | **The I-24 loader was written against the documented schema, not the file.** It assumed front-bumper positions, string ids and in-memory JSON. The real export has `{"$oid"}` ids, *back*-center feet, and is a 19.5 GB array. | Opening the file. | A streaming reader (never extracts the array), front-bumper conversion so the existing bumper-to-bumper gap logic holds, and loader tests whose expected gap changed from "spacing − leader length" to "spacing − follower length". | docs/I24_DATA.md §1, `calibration.loaders.i24motion`. |
| 7 | **Expecting whole trajectories from I-24 MOTION.** The roadmap expected far more clean episodes than NGSIM "from a modern instrument". | Every document is a fragment (median 117 m, 9.9 s); only 10.9% last 30 s. | Episode extraction on fragments as delivered, with documented masks for untracked leaders and duplicate fragments — and it still yielded 17,652 episodes, seven times NGSIM, with a better holdout RMSE. | docs/I24_DATA.md §2, §5. |
| 8 | **Treating fragment counts as traffic counts.** Peak-hour mainline crossings of 3,300 veh/h on four lanes at 30 km/h are physically too low. | Edie density against the calibrated equilibrium density at the observed speed: roughly half of vehicle-time is tracked in the peak. | Counts, flows and densities are lower bounds; speeds and wave speeds are trusted; demand is run in two labeled arms and the radar-detector counts are requested from the data owner. | docs/I24_DATA.md §4. |
| 9 | **`a_max` biased high blamed on raw-NGSIM noise.** | The I-24 fit on pipeline-smoothed positions gives the same `a_max` (1.06 vs 1.11 m/s²). | The M2 explanation was incomplete; the value is what gap-based calibration finds on two datasets. Recorded, not explained away. | docs/I24_DATA.md §5. |
| 10 | **Two spurious bottlenecks in the replica from SUMO's default lane-change rules.** At the Hickory Hollow diverge: 29.8 km/h over the kilometre upstream, 894 stalled samples in two hours. Through the Old Hickory merge: the two right lanes at 23–27 km/h while the left lanes ran at 65–97 km/h, 11,535 stalled samples — the data shows all four lanes equally loaded (30/24/20/26% of vehicle-time) and equally slow. | Tracing vehicles: exiting vehicles still in an inner lane stop at the diverge and wait for a gap (lane-change eagerness); the merge crawl comes from SUMO's keep-right obligation, which US freeways do not have. | `FleetSpec.lc_strategic` (5.0: 44 stalled samples, 84.7 km/h) and `FleetSpec.lc_keep_right` (0: 200 stalled samples, lane shares 32/26/22/20% against the observed 30/24/20/26%), same seed, same demand. Behaviour parameters calibrated on independent observables, exposed and documented rather than hidden in a route file. | docs/CONTRACTS.md §2; `scripts/i24_build_replica.py`; CHANGELOG. |
| 11 | **Pruning an OSM corridor by geometry-joined edge ids.** The first replica could not route: a joined edge's id names only one of its member ways. | SUMO's "no connection between edge … and …" at load. | Raw-way import mode (`osm_import(geometry_remove=False)`) to discover the ids a scenario must name. The gallery README had recorded the trap once already; it is now in code. | docs/I24_DATA.md §6. |
| 12 | **The wave detector above ~80 veh/km.** The absolute 40 km/h threshold labels a fully congested field as one jam and finds no fronts. | The ring diagnostic at 80–100 veh/km found zero fronts where stripes were visible. | Relative mode (`relative_frac × p90`), tested on a planted stripe in a 22 km/h field, labeled as a variant; the §7.1 criterion stays on the absolute threshold. | `validation.waves`, `artifacts/wave_speed_sitelength*.json`. |

| 13 | **Running the flagship validation like a small corridor.** Eight SUMO workers, a four-process LP bootstrap and parallel analysis workers together crashed a 16 GB machine twice. | The runner buffered every sampled trajectory row in Python lists until the run ended — several GB per worker on a 10 M-row run — and the LP and analysis processes each held gigabytes too. | Trajectories stream to Parquet in 500k-row groups during the run (content and byte-exact determinism unchanged); the validation runs with four workers and two analysis processes and nothing else concurrently; long jobs are launched one at a time. | CHANGELOG; `microsim.runner._TrajectoryWriter`. |
| 14 | **A results archive that only the exit path writes, in `/tmp`.** The 2026-09-06 cloud pipeline built its archive in its EXIT trap, into `/tmp`. The boot-time hard cap stopped the machine mid-stage; systemd gives a stopping unit seconds, Debian clears `/tmp` at boot, and the restart found nothing. | The 2-vCPU recovery restart: `scp: /tmp/final.tgz: No such file or directory`; the interim archive it built was cut off by the restart's own cap, and the truncated file held 8 of 11 artifacts. | `scripts/gcp/pipeline_i24.sh` archives to `$HOME` atomically after every stage, copies to a bucket when one is named, and traps SIGTERM; `watch_pipeline.sh` fetches whenever the archive changed. Nothing depends on the final fetch any more. | scripts/gcp/README.md (post-mortem), `artifacts/i24_validation_zip_*.json`. |
| 15 | **A hard cap shorter than the job.** The boot cap was set to 300 min from a two-hour estimate; the headway-cap sweep alone needed more than 2.4 h at 32 vCPUs, and the cap killed it. A cap is a backstop against a hung machine, not a schedule. | `lastStopTimestamp` 13:57 UTC = boot + 300 min, with `stage cap_sweep: start` the last pipeline line. | Default cap 480 min, documented as about twice the estimate; the stage-wise archive makes a cap kill lose one stage instead of the run. The cap sweep was rerun the same evening on a second VM, with the owner's approval and the summary written after every configuration. | scripts/gcp/launch_i24_pipeline.sh, CHANGELOG (Unreleased). |
| 16 | **Trusting a laptop-side timer as a billing failsafe.** The local watcher and a local delete timer both stalled while the MacBook slept (twice, hours each); a slow `gcloud describe` was one branch away from being read as "instance deleted". | The watcher's log gaps (10:00→12:34 and 16:45→19:47 UTC) and the recovery script's `Alarm clock` lines. | Every stop guarantee lives on the VM (boot cap, EXIT trap, optional self-delete after a bucket copy); the watcher runs under `caffeinate -i`, bounds every call, and confirms an empty status on the instance list before believing it. Local timers are conveniences, not guarantees. | scripts/gcp/watch_pipeline.sh, memory of the run in scripts/gcp/README.md. |
| 17 | **Commanding a speed through `vehicle.setSpeed` next to a stopped leader.** SUMO's speed influencer applies the safe-speed clamp first and the maximum-deceleration clamp last, so a commanded speed that needs more braking than the vehicle's comfortable `decel` is clipped to that deceleration and the vehicle drives into its leader. | The first scripted-merge draft: colliders decelerating at a constant 1.5 to 1.9 m/s² into a crawling leader on the acceleration lane (`meta.json` `collisions`, lane `102_0`). | Speed matching through the vehicle's desired speed (`setMaxSpeed`), which leaves the car-following model in charge of gaps and lane ends; zero collisions in every variant since. Every run now counts collisions in `meta.json` and the merge tests assert zero. | CHANGELOG 2026-09-16; `microsim.runner._scripted_merge_step`. |
| 18 | **Fixing the symptom at the entry.** The scripted late merge was designed to lift the replica's entry speed toward the observed 36 km/h, and it did (36 to 41 km/h); the ramp then admitted a quarter fewer vehicles and the held-out error rose. | `artifacts/i24_merge_experiment_scripted.json`: Old Hickory 1544 to 1638 of 2241 departed against the reference's 2066; RMSPE held-out 0.56 to 0.65 against 0.457. | A merge model is judged on admittance and the held-out hour together, never on the entry speed alone; the fitted arms keep the lane-change model and the model stays as an option for corridors whose observed behaviour is a forced late merge. | docs/I24_VALIDATION.md §0.8. |
| 19 | **Tooling defaults that drift.** The gcloud default project had silently changed to another of the owner's projects, and a bucket was created there; a launch script's shell expansion turned `gs://bucket` into `gs:` and aborted the launch after the VM was up, without a pipeline or a self-delete. | `gcloud config get-value project` printing an unrelated project; the launch log's `Received: "file://gs:"`. | The project is set explicitly before every gcloud command; the launch script parses the bucket root correctly and accepts `--allow-dirty`; the misplaced bucket was deleted within a minute and the orphan VM by hand. | CHANGELOG 2026-09-16; scripts/gcp/launch_i24_pipeline.sh. |
| 20 | **A documented behaviour nobody implemented.** `SimSpec.warmup_s` said "discarded from metrics" from the first schema; no metric, report or API path ever dropped it, so every published throughput, travel time and wave count included the warm-up (2 to 13% and +57% on the I-24 arms). | The second audit's validation lens grepped for the field's consumers and found none; a planted run confirmed the bias. | `compute_metrics(warmup_s=...)` windows every metric, the report states the window, and a warm-up that swallows the run raises. Published artifacts are marked as computed under the old definition until re-run. | CHANGELOG 2026-09-17; `validation.metrics.warmup_from_meta`. |
| 21 | **A tell that sat in the goldens for months.** Every micro golden carried `mean_tt_s == p90_tt_s`: the travel-time span ended at the farthest point any vehicle reached, so exactly one vehicle completed it and its time was published as a fleet mean with a confidence interval. | The audit read the golden files, not the code, and asked why a p90 equalled a mean. | The span ends at the median furthest position, the sample size is a field (`n_travel_time_veh`), and fewer than two completers gives NaN, never a "mean". Goldens regenerated with the reason in the changelog. | tests/golden/*.json history; `validation.metrics.default_travel_span`. |
| 22 | **Replying to a workflow agent by message.** A `SendMessage` to a workflow agent, whether it had finished or was mid-run asking a question, resumed a second copy of it outside the workflow that then edited the same files as the original. | Duplicate implementers on `frontend/src` (2026-09-16) and on `microsim/paths.py` (2026-09-17), each surprised by the other's edits. | Every decision goes into the agent's prompt up front; questions from workflow agents are answered by accepting their stated default, never by message; duplicates are stopped with TaskStop. | memory: autonomous-block-playbook. |
| 23 | **Three data-heavy agents on a 16 GB laptop.** An episode extraction over the 5 Hz I-24 file, an 8-process IDM fit, a report regeneration reading gigabytes of trajectories and a Node build ran at once; the machine froze and the owner had to force it off. Row 13 had recorded the same class of failure. | The owner's report; the interrupted agents' partial files in the tree. | Every job that reads large data or fits a population runs as a pipeline stage on a self-deleting VM; the laptop runs one light agent at a time, never a Node build beside a Python job. The whole evening round ran on the cloud. | memory rule of 2026-09-17; CHANGELOG. |
| 24 | **An archive rule written for one stage, reused for another.** The cloud archive listed the cap sweep's per-run metrics by name; the 500-run penetration sweep writes its metrics under a different tree and builds its summary with a separate script. The VM ran 6.5 hours, archived nothing of it and deleted itself. | `tar tzf final.tgz` of the 2026-09-18 sweep round: 60 cap-sweep metrics files, no `runs/i24_sweep`. | Before launching a stage, list what it writes and check each path against the archive rule; the sweep's metrics and manifest are archived and the analysis runs as a stage on the VM. | CHANGELOG 2026-09-18; scripts/gcp/pipeline_i24.sh. |
| 25 | **Trusting ramp detectors as demand.** MnDOT's ramp loops looked complete (13 of 17 ramps instrumented, every day present), yet three read zero on every day and one "exit" pair counted a collector–distributor split, more than the mainline. A demand built from them would have starved three entrances and drained the corridor at Mounds Blvd. | Mass-balance table between consecutive mainline stations in the session log; `artifacts/demand_mndot_i94_wb_stpaul.json` (`method` per ramp, `bracket_residuals`). | `scripts/corridor_demand.py` closes the mainline balance bracket by bracket and uses ramp detectors only for shares; a detector below 30 veh/h or above 90 % of the arriving flow is treated as dead; residuals are carried and listed, never hidden. | CHANGELOG 2026-09-23; docs/ONBOARDING_MNDOT.md §4–5. |
| 26 | **A fundamental diagram from 5-minute station means.** The congested branch of the flow–density cloud is flat once lanes and five minutes are averaged; the fit returned an implausible wave speed and jam density (−1.5 km/h and 1,025 veh/km in the session log; the rejected fit is not committed) and was rightly rejected by the plausibility check. | Two failed `fit_triangular_fd` calls on `detectors.csv` in the session log; the accepted fit's `source` field names per-lane 1-min samples. | The FD is fitted from per-lane 1-minute samples of the raw 30-s cache (300k rows, bootstrap CIs); the loader keeps that cache for this reason and the onboarding record says so. | `artifacts/fd_mndot_i94_wb_stpaul.json`. |
| 27 | **Logging estimated clock times.** During the 2026-09-23 block the coordinator wrote log stamps from its own estimate of elapsed time; they ran 1.5 hours ahead of the wall clock and would have made the block's report wrong about when the cloud round started. | `scratchpad/PRIORITIES_0922.md` log: stamps 00:27–02:21 corrected to ~00:20–00:44 after `date` showed 00:46. | Every log line takes its time from `date`; the block's report quotes only clock-sourced times. | memory: autonomous-block-playbook. |

| 31 | **A controller that ran once is not a controller that runs.** The ALINEA ramp meter had one single-seed experiment behind it; the first 20-seed strategy sweep that used it failed every ALINEA cell at the same vehicle, because the stop line sits 30 m before the end of a short ramp edge and SUMO refuses a stop a vehicle cannot brake for. | `logs/sweep_i24_strat.log` of the 2026-09-23 round (session record); CHANGELOG 2026-09-23 after 2.3.0. | A strategy enters a sweep grid only after a multi-seed smoke on the target corridor; the meter's stop placement must respect the vehicle's braking distance (backlog). | docs/I24_STRATEGIES.md. |

Two patterns run through the table. Most corrections came from reading the
primary source or the raw file instead of the summary of it (2, 6, 7). The rest
came from refusing to accept a number that violated physics (1, 8, 10) — the
same habit CLAUDE.md §0 demands of every headline result. Rows 14–16 and 19 are
operational rather than scientific: cloud runs whose stop guarantees held
and whose result guarantees, or tooling defaults, did not. Rows 17 and 18 are
the scripted merge: a simulator API subtlety, and a mechanism judged on the
wrong number. Rows 20 and 21 are the second audit's harvest: a documented
behaviour with no implementation, and a statistic whose own goldens gave it
away. Row 22 is about running the audits themselves.

OSM had the side right (`turn:lanes`, link geometry); nobody compared it with the compiled `fromLane`. | docs/ONBOARDING_MNDOT.md §9; `tests/test_microsim/test_microsim_osm_split_patch.py`; CHANGELOG 2026-09-24. | Every onboarded corridor gets a split audit (OSM side vs compiled lanes) beside the lane-profile check, and a diverge compiled on the wrong side is a build failure, not a modelling question; corrections go through `OSMNetwork.patch_files` and are pinned by a test on the committed extract. | docs/ONBOARDING_MNDOT.md §9. |
