# Weaving sections: a model for entrance lanes that also serve an exit

Design note written 2026-09-23 at 9738f2e (research and plan; no code). Every figure is from a named committed file; session-only figures are marked.

## 1. What a weaving section is, and why a lane-discrete merge locks there

HCM 7th ed. ch. 13 (Freeway Weaving Segments) defines a weave as two traffic streams crossing each other over a length of freeway without traffic control, created when an entrance is followed closely by an exit joined by an auxiliary lane. The controlling parameters are the short length L_S between the gore points, the configuration (how many lane changes each weaving movement *must* make — the HCM 2000 type A/B/C taxonomy, recast in the 6th/7th editions as one-sided vs two-sided with N_WL, the number of lanes from which a weave takes one change or none), the volume ratio, and the resulting lane-change density; speed is predicted separately for weaving and non-weaving streams, and capacity is reached when required lane-change density saturates.

Both MnDOT cases are one-sided ramp weaves (old type A: every weaving vehicle makes exactly one change, N_WL = 2). In `scenarios/mndot_i94_wb_stpaul.yaml` they are visible as an on-ramp and an off-ramp sharing an `attach_edge`: Ruth St (`on-ramp 745524613`) and the White Bear Ave exit (`off-ramp 18208090`) both on `999007700`, and T.H.52 (`on-ramp 769818012`) with the I-35E C-D exit (`off-ramp 18207598`) both on `51388891`. From the gore positions in `artifacts/demand_mndot_i94_wb_stpaul.json` the short lengths are 5,316.64 − 5,180.87 = **136 m** and 10,731.87 − 10,426.62 = **305 m** (detector-to-detector, `data/mndot/.../stations_x.csv`: 266 m and 581 m).

The lock has two layers. *Engine layer:* `microsim.networks.accel_lane_end` walks lane 0 downstream and refuses to terminate it when it feeds anything but the next corridor edge's lane 0 — the Ruth St case hits the `targets != [nxt]` branch, T.H.52 the `to_lanes != [0]` branch ("feeds both lanes of an exit piece", docs/ONBOARDING_MNDOT.md §6). Neither is a tail-length refusal: both are well inside `ACCEL_LANE_TAIL_MAX_M` = 400 m. So `_apply_merge_models` raises and every weave entrance is stuck on `lane_change`. (T.H.61 NB is the *other* branch — a 1,071 m added lane; its entrance detector `rnd_88807` has 2 lanes and the next station S1070 has 5 mainline lanes, so it is a lane addition/major merge, not a weave. It must not be counted as a weave success.) *Model layer:* under LC2013 both movements are mandatory changes against a deadline (lane end / gore). The entering vehicle waits for a gap in a lane occupied by exiting vehicles whose own mandatory change is into the lane the enterer is leaving; cooperative braking only slows the blocked vehicle; nobody creates the gap. In Treiber & Kesting's terms (MOBIL: incentive + politeness *p* + safety *b_safe*, plus a bias for mandatory changes) the exchange is symmetric and needs a two-sided incentive, which a lane-discrete deadline model does not supply. The result is a standing queue on the auxiliary lane — exactly the signature the I-24 flagship published: `artifacts/i24_lane_profile_zip.json` has the zipper family holding 17–33 % of vehicle-time in the acceleration lane at 4 km/h where the recording holds 6–8 % at 33–49 km/h, and the merge discharging ~5,880 veh/h against 6,630 observed (docs/I24_VALIDATION.md §0.11), an ~11 % deficit where GEH < 5 allows about 6 % (docs/MERGE_ROUND6_PLAN.md §1).

## 2. Three candidates, ranked by fit

**(A) `RampSpec.merge: "weave"` + `WeaveSpec`, two-sided gap acceptance — recommended.** Config (`packages/flowstate_core/flowstate_core/config.py`): add `"weave"` to the `merge` literal and `weave: WeaveSpec | None` with `exit_ramp: str` (the paired off-ramp's `name`), `length_m: float | None` (else measured from the network), and `weave_params: dict[str, float]` validated against a `WEAVE_DEFAULTS` mirroring `SCRIPTED_MERGE_DEFAULTS` (`accept_gap_s`, `force_after_s`, `force_within_m`, `change_duration_s`, `lookahead_m`, `courtesy`, plus `exit_accept_gap_s`), both fields entering the config hash. Runner: `_apply_merge_models` skips termination *and* patches for `weave` (lane 0 must stay connected to the exit) and validates the pairing; `_scripted_merge_step` (runner.py:622–703) generalises to a direction argument — entering vehicles change left as today, exiting vehicles on lane 1 change right using `NEIGHBOR_RIGHT_*` gaps, with `courtesy` as MOBIL's politeness on both sides, and forcing allowed only for the exiting movement (its deadline is real). The exiting set is free: `route_by_id` (runner.py:1179) already carries `"*_off<j>"`. Trade-offs: TraCI-only, no netconvert, reuses a tested controller and its `meta.json` reporting; but it is a heuristic with new parameters and needs care that forced two-way changes do not produce collisions. **Cost ≈ 6–8 engineering days.**

**(B) Network-level split (merge piece + exit piece).** New `weave_patch_files` splitting the shared edge and re-connecting lane 0 so a merge taper dead-ends and a separate exit taper is fed from lane 1. `--ramps.guess` cannot produce this: it adds one `-AddedOnRampEdge` / `-AddedOffRampEdge` per ramp and, when both sit on one edge, leaves lane 0 continuous (the T.H.52 refusal message is precisely that). Mechanically it fits — `patch_net`, `expand_ramp_splits` and the compiled-id remapping all exist — but it forbids the exchange HCM says defines the section (a Ruth St vehicle could no longer reach White Bear Ave), deletes capacity, and pushes the same lock one edge upstream. Useful as a diagnostic bound only. **≈ 3–4 days.**

**(C) Sublane model on the weave edge.** `SimSpec.lateral_resolution_m` plus `FleetSpec.lc_sublane` / `lat_alignment` / `lc_pushy` / `min_gap_lat` already exist. Lowest fit: `SimSpec` is run-wide, not per-edge, so it changes every merge and every config hash, and I-24 excluded it (insertion 0.4299–0.4412 against the reference's 0.9349, docs/MERGE_ROUND6_PLAN.md §1; gridlock at defaults, §0.5(h)). **≈ 0.5 day** — run it *first* as the free falsifier, then discard.

**Tests (all three).** A planted-weave fixture beside `RAMP_OSM` / `SPILL_OSM` in `tests/test_microsim/test_microsim_osm_ramps.py`: an on-ramp at the attach edge and a `motorway_link` leaving ~150 m downstream. Unit: `accel_lane_end` still refuses (the existing "weaving section" assertion at `test_microsim_merge_managed_meter.py:434` becomes the positive detector); a new `weave_sections()` pairs the ramps and reports L_S. Run-level: both movements complete (`n_unfinished == 0`, off-ramp arrivals ≈ planned, `meta["n_collisions"] == 0`), determinism (`test_microsim_determinism.py`), a `tests/golden/merge_weave.json` beside `merge_scripted.json`, and schema rejections (weave on an off-ramp, unpaired exit, unknown `weave_params`).

**MnDOT validation, staged with deciders fixed now.** (1) Local 35-min peak slice, one seed: both weave entrances deliver ≥ 95 % of demand *and* their exits arrive within 5 % of planned. (2) Full 4-hour single seed: departed fraction ≥ 0.9 against round 2's 0.4062 [0.3911, 0.4210] of 34,367 planned (`round2_gridlock_record.json`); T.H.52 (peak 1,419 veh/h) rises from 2,078/5,047; Ruth St holds at 995/995; T.H.61 NB (2,196 veh/h) is *expected not to move* — it is the lane-addition case. (3) 20 seeds, ≈ 2,780 s wall per replicate on an n2-standard-32 at ≈ $1.55/h (docs/MERGE_ROUND6_PLAN.md §2), i.e. about one machine-hour, ≈ $1.6; both earlier rounds together cost ≈ $3.3.

## 4. What evidence would show the model is right

1. **I-24 is the falsification control, not the target.** Old Hickory's acceleration lane serves no exit, so a correct weave model leaves the flagship byte-identical: the discharge stays ~5,880 veh/h and the published rows do not move (§0.11). The transferable positive signature is the lane profile — the auxiliary lane must hold a small share of vehicle-time at high speed (recording: 6–8 % at 33–49 km/h), not 17–33 % at 4 km/h.
2. **MnDOT departed fractions** (`round2_gridlock_record.json`): the corridor mean rises from 0.4062 toward ≥ 0.9; T.H.52 from 0.41 and the downtown approach from 0.25 rise; Ruth St stays at 1.00; T.H.61 NB stays near 0.18 until the lane-addition case is built — a weave model that "fixes" T.H.61 NB is fixing the wrong geometry.
3. **Round-2 first-seed station speeds** (`round2_first_seed_station_speeds.json`, RMSPE 0.901, GEH pass 0.0): simulated means of 0.28–1.85 m/s at every station must be replaced by the observed *shape* — free flow at S1063–S1066 (31.83, 29.81, 30.51, 28.47 m/s), onset at S1067/S1068 (22.46, 14.16), minimum at S1069 (8.49), recovery at S97 (15.95). Round 1 (free flow everywhere, GEH < 5 on 17 % of 840 link-hours, RMSPE 93 %, mid-span throughput 1,805 [1,798, 1,812] veh/h against 2,900–3,000 observed) and round 2 (gridlock everywhere) bracket the two ways to be wrong.
4. **Pre-registered falsifiers:** departed fraction ≥ 0.9 with every station above 20 m/s = the round-1 failure recreated; `n_forced` dominating `n_changed_out` = a teleport, not gap acceptance; any `n_collisions > 0` = reject. The last-attempt result — merge waits 7.2 → 4.4 s with downstream speeds unchanged at 3.4, 1.0, 1.7 m/s (docs/ONBOARDING_MNDOT.md §6, session record, not committed) — is the precedent that a merge-side-only improvement proves nothing.

## 2026-09-24 (block 3): the T.H.52 fixture, the mechanism, and a re-derivation that did not work

**Fixture and test.** `tests/fixtures/weave_th52.osm` is the T.H.52 geometry (docs/ONBOARDING_MNDOT.md §10) in the shape of `weave.osm`: ways 100–101 (3 lanes, 583 m compiled), 102 (4 lanes, 308 m compiled from 267 m of node spacing — netconvert adds the junction geometry), 103–104 (3 lanes, ~600 m), entrance 200 into lane 0 of 102, exit 201 from it. `TestWeaveRun::test_th52_weave_at_capacity_flows` runs mainline 4,500 veh/h with 25 % exiting and 1,400 veh/h entering for 20 min at 0.5 s, fleet defaults, seed 3 (~8 s wall), and asks for a flowing section: lane 1 over the first 60 m above 5 m/s in every 60-s window after 120 s, ≥ 90 % of the entrance's plan departed, ≤ 10 % of driven vehicles unfinished, no collision. It is `xfail(strict=True)`. At 462b731 lane 1 reads 20.2, 10.9, 2.9, 0.2 m/s in minutes 0–3 and 0.0 from minute 4; the entrance departs 81 of 466; 92 vehicles are driven, 25 change in, 18 out, 0 forced, 16,576 forced changes deferred, 49 unfinished; 3 of the 62 exit-bound vehicles that reach the section exit; 583 of 1,966 planned vehicles depart; no collision. At two thirds of that demand (3,000 / 900 veh/h, 10 min) the same rules crawl instead of locking: 21.5, 18.6, 8.9, 4.3, 4.4, 2.3, 2.3, 1.9, 2.7, 4.0, 4.7 m/s.

**Mechanism, from a per-step trace on the fixture (seed 3; `_weave_step` wrapped, every `setMaxSpeed`/`changeLane`/`setLaneChangeMode` logged with `getNeighbors` gaps).** (1) The first standstill is in the *middle* through lane: at t = 55.5 s exit-bound v00016 in lane 2 at x = 228 m (just inside the last 80 m) receives `setMaxSpeed(0.0)` from the station-keeping rule — it holds `2 s0 + accept · v` behind whatever `getNeighbors` reports ahead in the lane to its right, which for a lane-2 vehicle is a lane-1 vehicle, with no floor — while its forced change is deferred because lane 1 beside it is a slow queue; through vehicle v00019 stops 0.2 m behind it and a vehicle sits at 0.0 m/s at x = 229–242 m from t = 60 to 84 s while lane 2 queues upstream. (2) Lanes 0 and 1 are coupled by speed matching in one direction only: an entering vehicle takes the speed of its lane-1 leader (`v_match`) along the whole auxiliary lane and an exiting vehicle in the zone the speed of (or a station behind) its lane-0 leader, so every disturbance ratchets both lanes down. (3) Lane 1's first stop in the section's first 60 m (t = 116 s) is exit-bound v00105 at x = 29 m braking at −8 m/s²: the exchange rule had dropped it to 4 m/s as the rear vehicle of an exchange, and entering v00337, matched to a 3.2 m/s lane-1 leader, executed its accepted change under mode 256 at 2.9 m/s directly ahead of it. Not the cause: the gap parameters (§10 probe), SUMO's cooperative model (driven vehicles run under modes 512/256 with every model-driven change off; the hard braking of through vehicles in the traces is IDM car-following behind inserted or stopped vehicles), the exit link (lane 0's queue head discharges freely).

**The re-derivation.** The weave as a two-sided zipper: (R1) an exiting vehicle drives with its own lane until the last `force_within_m`, an entering vehicle rolls down the auxiliary lane at the speed of lane 1's vehicle ahead and never below a floor; (R2) a committed changer positions itself against the target lane — first centred in the nearest gap (±2 m/s), then only clear of its target-lane leader by `2 s0 + accept · closing`; (R3) an exiting vehicle holds station behind a *driven entering* vehicle ahead in lane 0, the one place the floor is zero, so the pair never arrives abreast at the gore; (R4) every change is a one-step mode-256 execution through `_weave_force_gap_ok` — accepted gaps, or the guard alone once forcing is due — never a mode-512 request (SUMO answers one by adapting the requester's speed to a gap of its own choosing); (R5) a due forced change whose only obstacle is the target-lane follower asks that follower to open the gap. Eight forms were run on the fixture (session record; no code ships): R1+R4 alone locked all lanes by minute 3 (67 unfinished, 37,645 deferred) — the abreast-at-the-gore deadlock, entrant v00196 held by SUMO at the end of lane 0 (x = 308 m; its route continues on 103) and exiter v00019 creeping up lane 1 at the 3 m/s floor to x = 307.7 m beside it; adding R3 and matching along the lane locked by minute 3 with 0 forced changes because matching freezes the relative position (v00016 overlapped the same lane-0 vehicle by 2.5 m for 13 s); adding centring and R5 locked by minute 3 (25,542 deferred); making the floors relative (zero absolute) gave a 1–2 m/s crawl for ten minutes and a lock at minute 11 (69 + 43 forced, mean wait 104 s) — a ratchet, since each changer's reference is a changer further ahead; clear-of-leader positioning removed the lock but left lanes 0–2 at 0.3–2.5 m/s to the end (entrance 135 of 466) because entrants outside the zone were commanded 0.0 m/s beside a 1.5 m/s lane-1 queue with 20–60 m of free lane ahead; a creep floor outside the zone only gave a 1 m/s crawl throughout (entrance 207 of 466, 121 in / 175 out, waits 171 s / 120 s); rate-limiting every command (3 m/s²) and yield (2 m/s²) and adding a 0.5 s time gap to the forced guard — the shocks they remove are real (a through vehicle arriving at 30.9 m/s cut to a 6.5 m/s cap braked at −9 m/s² at t = 45.5 s; a 2 m follower gap is an emergency brake to IDM) — locked hard by minute 3 with 0 forced and 6,513 yield steps, a stopped changer and its stopped follower too close to open; letting entrants force by waiting time anywhere on the lane locked at minute 6. Applied singly to 462b731, station-keeping only behind driven entrants and the rate limits each turned the two-thirds-demand crawl into a lock (minutes 5 and 9), and together with the guard they failed `test_moderate_crossing_demand_does_not_crawl` (12 unfinished). The runner is therefore byte-identical to 462b731 and the golden `merge_weave` unchanged.

**What the fixture establishes for the next attempt.** (a) Once changes happen only in the last `force_within_m`, lane 0 there carries entrants and exiters together — 2,525 veh/h against a lane's ≈ 2,050 veh/h at T = 1.4 s — so a jam formed at the gore never discharges; the changes must complete upstream, at speed. (b) An accepted change at the 0.6 s gap makes an IDM follower with T = 1.4 s brake at ≈ 4 m/s² (v00012, t = 40 s, −4.4 m/s² behind an entrant 24 m ahead); at 1,400 insertions/h lane 1 is compressed into platoons with no acceptable hole within a minute, which is what sends entrants to the gore. (c) A vehicle stopped at the gore cannot enter a 20 m/s stream under any guard. Together these say a saturated weave needs the through lane's *anticipatory* cooperation — a follower opening a gap at 1–2 m/s² several seconds before the changer is beside it — which neither LC2013 under the TraCI modes nor a max-speed cap provides (a cap below the current speed is an emergency brake to IDM's free term). The candidate is a virtual-leader car-following target for the cooperating follower (the entrant's projected position in the follower's own lane), with acceptance at the follower's own headway rather than a fixed 0.6 s; it is untested. Session artifacts (trace harness, per-attempt runs) were not committed.

## 2026-09-24 (block 3, second attempt): follower cooperation as a car-following target — no lock, a crawl

**What ships** (`microsim.runner._weave_step` and helpers `_idm_accel`, `_weave_veh`, `_weave_lane_map`, `_weave_choose_gap`, `_weave_command`, `_weave_cooperate`; docs/CONTRACTS.md §2). The max-speed caps are gone: no speed matching, no station-keeping, no exchange rule, `courtesy` inert. Every driven vehicle keeps SUMO's own car-following under mode 512; every speed request is a one-step target `vehicle.slowDown(v, 0.0)` (duration 0 is the one-step form on SUMO 1.27.1 — a duration of one step lingers two steps, half-way then full; and under the default `speedMode` the target is clamped to the safe speed and to the vehicle's `decel`, so neither a collision nor a brake beyond b can be commanded). (1) *Gap choice*: a changer lists the target lane on the section axis (section edges, the through lanes of the corridor edge before and after, the on-ramp at negative positions) and picks, among the gaps it is in or abreast of within `lookahead_m` behind it, the nearest one whose follower F can open it at ≤ b_F (IDM towards the changer as virtual leader, F's own `tau/accel/decel/minGap/maxSpeed`), gaps it can also follow into first; the commitment is kept while F still opens it within b_F. Ranking by "least deceleration" alone was rejected on paper: the farthest follower always wins and the change is made at the gore. (2) *Cooperation*: F is driven at min(own IDM, a_F clipped at −b_F); several changers on one follower keep the lowest target. (3) *Easing*: the changer gets the same target towards its gap's leader when it would have to brake for it; only the rear one of an abreast pair has such a gap. (4) *Anticipation*: an entrant on the ramp within `lookahead_m` of the section chooses its gap, its follower cooperates and it eases before it appears on lane 0. (5) Both movements are forced in the last `force_within_m` after `force_after_s`, under the guard. (6) Acceptance = time gaps (`accept_gap_s`) + the immediate follower absorbing the changer within its b + the guard, executed under mode 256 for one step. New `meta.json` counters: `n_cooperations`, `mean_follower_decel_ms2`, `n_changer_eased`.

**Fixture numbers, seed 3, before → after.** Lane 1 over the first 60 m, minutes 2–19: 20.2, 10.9, 2.9, 0.2 then 0.0 → 12.7, 11.8, 12.4, 10.3, 5.8, 5.9, 4.9, 6.4, 6.3, 5.4, 6.2, 4.8, 5.5, 5.2, 5.4, 4.6, 4.2, 2.3 m/s. **What was tried and rejected on the fixture (session record, same seed).** Without the abreast tie-break, an exiter 4.5 m ahead of an entrant and the entrant each took the other as their gap's leader and both braked at b to a standstill (t = 55–70 s at x ≈ 85–90 m). Without ramp anticipation, a through vehicle at 28.7 m/s met an entrant appearing 7 m ahead at 19 m/s, overshot to 8 m behind, and the pair rode abreast to the gore where SUMO's lane-end braking took the entrant to 0 and the follower tracked it down (t = 30–45 s). Without easing anywhere (the task's literal rule), abreast pairs never resolve and every entrant reaches the gore, where the cooperating follower stops behind it and the guard refuses the forced change: 33,876 deferred, 56 unfinished, every lane at 0 from minute 3; a bumper-to-bumper guard did not change that. Easing off on the ramp only: 3–4 m/s throughout. `lookahead_m` 60: 4–5 m/s. An "exiter yields" tie-break instead of "rear yields": 3–4 m/s. No entrant forcing / bumper guard: byte-identical to the shipped form (neither binds).

**What stops it now — a crawl equilibrium at the section entry, not a lock.** Per-lane flows (veh/h at mean speed), window 120–360 s: x = −50 m lane 1: 705 @ 8.5 m/s while lanes 2, 3 carry 1,455 @ 16.8 and 1,440 @ 18.8; x = 20 m lane 0 (entrants): 570 @ 12.1, lane 1: 1,110 @ 11.4; x = 290 m: 1,140 / 735 / 1,065 / 1,380; x = 500 m (three lanes): 720 / 1,155 / 1,425 — a total discharge of 3,300 veh/h against 5,900 of demand. Window 600–1,200 s: x = −50 m: 522 @ 2.5, 960 @ 3.2, 1,152 @ 4.8; x = 20 m: 438 @ 4.4 / 804 @ 4.2 / 822 @ 4.2 / 1,050 @ 5.8; x = 150 m all lanes ≈ 13 m/s; x = 290 m ≈ 18 m/s; total ≈ 2,500 veh/h. The section itself accelerates from x ≈ 20 m on; the choke is at x = 0–20 m, where the lane-1 followers cooperating for entrants and the entrants easing for lane-1 leaders settle both lanes at the slowest element (each is an IDM target towards a vehicle in the other lane, bounded at b but with no floor), and exit-bound vehicles in lanes 2–3 then stop at the end of edge 101 for SUMO's strategic change into the crawling lane 1 (t = 400 s: 101 lane 1 at 0.2 m/s 13 m before the section, lane 2 at 0.7 m/s at 29 m), which spreads the queue to every lane. The combined stream at the entry (1,500 veh/h in lane 1 + 1,400 entrants) exceeds one IDM lane's ≈ 2,050 veh/h at T = 1.4 s, so a cooperation that zips them at the gore cannot carry it; the exchange that would (exiters vacate lane 1 upstream, entrants take the holes, net lane-1 flow ≈ 1,775 veh/h) needs entrants to spread along the 308 m and through vehicles to leave lane 1 — under a crawl SUMO's LC2013 sees no speed gain and nobody moves left (x = −50 m: 21 vehicles in lane 1, 6 and 5 in lanes 2 and 3 at t = 200 s). Candidates for a third attempt: a target-lane occupancy term in the gap choice (prefer the hole an exiter is leaving, defer the change while lane 1 is denser than lane 2), a floor on the cooperation target tied to the lane's flow, and a cheaper cooperation (F opens to the comfort boundary, not to full IDM spacing, then lets its own model finish after the change).
